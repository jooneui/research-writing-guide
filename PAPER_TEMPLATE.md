# Research paper scaffold

- **Venue or advisor rules:** [insert]
- **Research question:** [insert]
- **Why this question matters:** [what changes in understanding or decision if answered]
- **Main claim or working hypothesis:** [precise, bounded statement]
- **Key terms:** [one label and definition per concept]
- **Citation style:** [required style]

| Section | Purpose | Why this purpose matters | Deliverable | Claim / reason / evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Introduction | Establish context, the unresolved question, and the main claim | Show why answering the question matters | Scope and contribution | Main claim; preview of reasons |
| Background | Define terms and prior work | Give readers the comparison basis | Definitions and relevant sources | Cite claims about prior work |
| Method / model | Show how the question is tested | Make results interpretable and reproducible | Assumptions, equations, data, procedure | Evidence for method choice and limits |
| Results | Report observations | Answer the research question | Figures, tables, estimates | One claim, reason, and evidence chain per finding |
| Discussion | Interpret and test the claims | Explain meaning and bounds | Alternatives, limitations, comparison with prior work | Address objections and conflicting evidence |
| Conclusion | State the supported answer | Show what the answer adds | Bounded conclusion and next question | No new unsupported claim |

Within a section, use the purpose, why, and expected deliverables to plan its opening. Use a subsection for each deliverable when several outputs need separate treatment. Present each completed finding from those outputs with a claim, reason, and evidence. In the finished paper, express this structure in prose unless the venue requests labels.

For each results paragraph, draft this compact check before writing prose:

- **(Claim)** [What the result shows, including conditions.]
- **(Reason)** [Why the observed pattern supports that claim.]
- **(Evidence)** [Specific figure/table value or source, with units and uncertainty.]
- **(Limit)** [Relevant alternative or boundary.]

For each figure, check: question or point; matched definitions and units; visible result; caption; source; interpretation in the text. A side-by-side comparison also needs a common basis and a note on any unmatched assumptions.

Before finalizing a claim, ask: Does the evidence support its reason? Why does that reason support the claim? What plausible alternative or exception would change the conclusion?
